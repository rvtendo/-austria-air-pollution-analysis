Land Cover Changes From 2021 to 2023

Land Cover Change Statistical Report

This section presents the area-based land cover change statistics between 2021 and 2023. The results were calculated using area values in square meters (m²), instead of pixel percentages, to provide a more accurate representation of the actual extent of land cover changes across the study area.

This map illustrates land cover transitions observed in Austria between 2021 and 2023. Most areas remained stable throughout the study period, while the detected changes mainly occurred between rangeland, cropland, tree cover, and built-up areas.

To obtain accurate area measurements, the Land Cover Changes Map from 2021 to 2023 was reprojected to a metric coordinate system (EPSG:3577) using the Warp (Reproject) tool in QGIS. Following the reprojection process, a raster value report was generated and the resulting area values were exported to Excel for statistical analysis.

NO₂ Interpretation

    A large proportion of the study area falls within the **11 and 21 classes**, indicating that both **NO₂ concentrations and population density are generally low to moderate across most parts of Austria**. However, the districts of **Vienna and Salzburg** stand out, as they exhibit **higher population densities accompanied by relatively elevated NO₂ exposure levels**. This pattern suggests that urbanized areas tend to experience greater air pollution exposure, likely due to increased transportation activity and human-related emissions.

PM10 Interpretation

    The 11 class is the most dominant category in the PM10 bivariate map, indicating that both **population density and PM10 concentrations are generally low across most regions of Austria**. The **21 and 22 classes**, observed particularly in the districts of **Vienna and Salzburg**, represent areas with moderate levels of population exposure to PM10 pollution. A small number of areas fall into the 15 class, which indicates relatively high PM10 concentrations despite low population density. Overall, PM10 pollution does not appear to be widespread across Austria, as the majority of districts are characterized by low exposure levels and limited spatial concentration of this pollutant.

PM2.5 Interpretation

    The PM2.5 bivariate map exhibits a greater diversity of classes compared to the other pollutants. Most districts fall within the 21 and 31 classes, indicating low to moderate levels of population exposure to PM2.5 pollution. The city of Vienna is classified as 35, representing an area where both population density and PM2.5 concentrations are relatively high. In contrast, Salzburg falls within the 25 class, indicating a lower level of exposure risk compared to Vienna. These results suggest that PM2.5 exposure is more concentrated in densely populated urban areas, while most regions of Austria experience relatively moderate exposure levels.
