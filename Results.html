Our Research Findings

This section presents the results obtained from the air pollution analysis conducted using GIS and WebGIS technologies. The outputs include spatial distribution maps of NO₂, PM10, and PM2.5, land cover change detection results, and population exposure analysis. These visualizations provide insights into atmospheric pollution patterns and their potential relationship with land cover dynamics and population distribution across the study area.

Results

Annual Mean Aggregation Change (AMAC) Analysis

Air quality due to their significant impacts on human health and the environment. In this study, annual mean concentration data for these pollutants were used to investigate spatiotemporal changes across Austria between 2021 and 2023.Monthly raster datasets for each pollutant were obtained from the Copernicus Atmosphere Monitoring Service (CAMS) European Air Quality Reanalysis database. The downloaded datasets were processed and clipped to the Austrian national boundary using QGIS. All analyses were conducted using the EPSG:4326 (WGS 84) coordinate reference system. For each pollutant, annual mean concentration rasters were generated by averaging the twelve monthly datasets corresponding to each year. Subsequently, the resulting annual mean rasters were classified according to threshold values defined by European Union air quality standards. To evaluate temporal changes in air pollution levels, Annual Mean Aggregation Change (AMAC) maps were produced by subtracting the 2021 annual mean raster from the corresponding 2023 raster.

NO₂ Interpretation

  • Most areas of Austria showed little or no change in NO₂ concentrations between 2021 and 2023.
  • Some decreases can be observed in the eastern part of the country, indicating slight improvements in air quality.
  • A few areas in central Austria experienced moderate increases in NO₂ levels.
  • Overall, NO₂ changes were localized and no widespread increase was observed across the country.

PM10 Interpretation

  • PM10 was the most stable pollutant among the three analyzed pollutants.
  • Most of the study area showed almost no change between 2021 and 2023.
  • Only a few small areas experienced slight increases in PM10 concentrations.
  • Overall, PM10 levels remained largely unchanged throughout the study period.

PM2.5 Interpretation

  • PM2.5 concentrations remained relatively stable across most of Austria during the study period.
  • Decreases were mainly observed in the northern regions, while some increases appeared in the south.
  • The changes were generally small and limited to specific locations.
  • These results suggest that PM2.5 levels did not change significantly at the national scale.

The AMAC analysis indicates that air quality conditions in Austria remained generally stable between 2021 and 2023. Among the three pollutants, NO₂ exhibited the highest spatial variability, with both localized increases and decreases observed across the country. PM2.5 showed a similar pattern but with more limited changes, while PM10 remained the most stable pollutant throughout the study period. Overall, the detected changes were concentrated in specific regions rather than being widespread, suggesting that air pollution dynamics during the study period were primarily driven by local factors rather than large-scale national trends.

Land Cover Change Detection

Land cover change detection analysis revealed significant transitions in land surface characteristics between 2021 and 2023. Notable changes included urban expansion, deforestation, and agricultural land conversion. These land cover dynamics were spatially correlated with observed air pollution patterns, suggesting potential interactions between land use changes and atmospheric pollutant distribution.

Area-Based Land Cover Change Statistics

The second dataset used in this study was the Land Cover data obtained from the ArcGIS Living Atlas of the World. The tiles corresponding to the study area for the years 2021 and 2023 were downloaded and clipped to the Austrian boundary in QGIS. Similar to the air pollution analysis, the EPSG:4326 (WGS 84) coordinate reference system was used during the preprocessing stage.A land cover change detection raster was generated using the Raster Calculator tool by comparing the 2021 and 2023 land cover datasets. Based on the resulting change raster, a statistical report was produced to analyze stable, gained, and lost areas for the selected land cover classes, namely Built Areas, Trees, and Crops. Cloud-covered areas (Land Cover Class 10) were excluded from the analysis to avoid introducing uncertainty into the results.To enable accurate area calculations, the dataset was reprojected from EPSG:4326 to a metric coordinate reference system, EPSG:3577 (GDA94 / Australian Albers), allowing area values to be calculated in square meters (m²). The resulting statistics were then used to generate area-based reports, and the final results were summarized and presented in tabular form.

Interpretation

The results indicate that Austria's land cover remained largely unchanged during the study period, with only localized transitions observed among vegetation and agricultural land classes. Although some urban growth was detected, the dominant changes were associated with natural vegetation dynamics and agricultural land-use conversions.

Trees

  • Tree cover was the most stable class with 96.34% persistence.
  • Most gains originated from Rangeland (65.68%).
  • Cropland was the second largest source of gain (24.35%).
  • Most losses transitioned to Rangeland (80.73%).
  • Forest ecosystems remained highly stable between 2021 and 2023.

Crops

  • Cropland remained highly stable with 95.50% persistence.
  • Most gains came from Rangeland (58.56%).
  • Built Areas contributed 20.06% of cropland gains.
  • Most losses were converted to Trees (42.89%).
  • The results indicate strong interaction between agriculture and natural vegetation.

Built Areas

  • Built Areas showed 93.57% persistence.
  • Urban growth mainly occurred on Cropland (56.04%).
  • Tree Cover and Rangeland were secondary sources of gain.
  • Most built-area losses transitioned back to Cropland (58.18%).
  • Urban expansion was present but relatively limited.

Zonal Statistics

The generated land cover concentration map was processed using the Raster Calculator to create three separate mask layers corresponding to Built Areas, Crops, and Trees. Zonal Statistics was then applied to each mask layer to calculate the mean, minimum, and maximum values of pollutant concentration change. The resulting statistics were visualized through bar charts and analyzed to evaluate the relationship between land cover dynamics and changes in air pollutant concentrations.

The zonal statistics reveal a consistent decrease in pollutant concentrations across all land cover change categories. For NO₂, PM10, and PM2.5, the negative mean values indicate that air quality generally improved between 2021 and 2023.

NO₂ Interpretation

  • The mean NO₂ change values are negative across all zones (Stable, Gain, and Loss), indicating an overall reduction in NO₂ concentrations between 2021 and 2023.
  • The largest average decrease was observed in Stable areas (-2.07 µg/m³).
  • Loss areas recorded the highest maximum value (2.38 µg/m³), suggesting that some localized increases in NO₂ concentrations occurred.
  • Overall, NO₂ levels generally declined across the study area, with only limited areas showing increases.

PM10 Interpretation

  • PM10 also shows negative mean values in all zones, indicating an overall improvement in air quality.
  • The greatest average reduction occurred in Gain areas (-1.49 µg/m³).
  • The lowest minimum value (-3.59 µg/m³) was observed in Loss areas, reflecting significant decreases in PM10 concentrations in some locations.
  • Although a few areas experienced increases, the dominant trend was a decrease in PM10 levels.

PM2.5 Interpretation

  • All mean PM2.5 values are negative, indicating a general reduction in PM2.5 concentrations between 2021 and 2023.
  • The largest average decrease was found in Loss areas (-1.08 µg/m³).
  • Positive maximum values (up to 0.92 µg/m³) indicate that some locations experienced slight increases in PM2.5 concentrations.
  • Nevertheless, the overall pattern suggests an improvement in PM2.5 air quality across all land cover change categories.

Bivariate Population Exposure Analysis

The third dataset used in this study was the population data obtained from WorldPop.org. The population raster was classified into five density classes and combined with the 2023 Pollutant Concentration Map to produce a Bivariate Map.This map highlights the spatial relationship between population density and pollutant concentrations, helping to identify areas where both variables are high. The legend is presented as a 5 × 5 matrix, where one axis represents population density and the other represents pollutant concentration levels. Darker colors indicate areas with both higher population density and higher pollution levels, while lighter colors represent lower values of both variables.

When the three maps are evaluated together, it can be observed that most of Austria's population is exposed to low to moderate levels of air pollution. The highest exposure classes are concentrated in the districts of Wien and Salzburg, which are also among the most densely populated urban areas in the country.Among the pollutants analyzed, PM2.5 shows the strongest association with population density, with the highest exposure levels occurring in densely populated regions. Overall, the results suggest that pollution-related health risks are not widespread across Austria; however, certain urban centers may require closer monitoring due to their relatively higher exposure levels.

NO₂ Interpretation

    A large proportion of the study area falls within the 11 and 21 classes, indicating that both NO₂ concentrations and population density are generally low to moderate across most parts of Austria. However, the districts of Wine and Salzburg stand out, as they exhibit higher population densities accompanied by relatively elevated NO₂ exposure levels. This pattern suggests that urbanized areas tend to experience greater air pollution exposure, likely due to increased transportation activity and human-related emissions.

PM10 Interpretation

    The 11 class is the most dominant category in the PM10 bivariate map, indicating that both population density and PM10 concentrations are generally low across most regions of Austria. The 21 and 22 classes, observed particularly in the districts of Vienna and Salzburg, represent areas with moderate levels of population exposure to PM10 pollution. A small number of areas fall into the 15 class, which indicates relatively high PM10 concentrations despite low population density. Overall, PM10 pollution does not appear to be widespread across Austria, as the majority of districts are characterized by low exposure levels and limited spatial concentration of this pollutant.

PM2.5 Interpretation

    The PM2.5 bivariate map exhibits a greater diversity of classes compared to the other pollutants. Most districts fall within the 21 and 31 classes, indicating low to moderate levels of population exposure to PM2.5 pollution. The city of Vienna is classified as 35, representing an area where both population density and PM2.5 concentrations are relatively high. In contrast, Salzburg falls within the 25 class, indicating a lower level of exposure risk compared to Vienna. These results suggest that PM2.5 exposure is more concentrated in densely populated urban areas, while most regions of Austria experience relatively moderate exposure levels.

Proportion of Population Exposure Classes for Pollutant

The Level 2 administrative boundary dataset of Austria, obtained from the FAO GAUL database, was used together with the population dataset to analyze population exposure to air pollution. Using the Zonal Statistics tool, population data were combined with the classified pollutant concentration maps to identify the distribution of population exposure across different pollution classes.The resulting dataset was subsequently processed using the Dissolve operation to aggregate areas belonging to the same exposure category. Based on these results, Proportion of Population Exposure Classes for Pollutants charts were generated to illustrate the percentage of the population exposed to different levels of air pollution across Austria.

Interpretation

The population exposure charts indicate that PM10 exposure is generally low across Austria, with nearly 80% of the population belonging to the lowest exposure class. NO₂ exposure is relatively uniform, with most residents concentrated in a single exposure category. In contrast, PM2.5 shows the highest population exposure levels, with more than half of the population falling into the higher exposure class. Overall, the results suggest that PM2.5 may represent the most significant air quality concern among the pollutants analyzed.
